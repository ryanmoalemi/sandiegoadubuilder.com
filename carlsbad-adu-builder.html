What Carlsbad actually allows in 2026

The City Council adopted Ordinance CS-506 on February 3, 2026, by a 5-0 vote. It amends Carlsbad Municipal Code section 21.10.030. Outside the coastal zone it took effect 30 days after adoption. Inside the coastal zone it takes effect on the later of that 30 days or Coastal Commission approval of Local Coastal Program Amendment LCPA 2025-0023. We have not confirmed that certification. If your lot is coastal, ask Planning which text applies today.

The ordinance text for a new attached ADU says 50 percent of the main dwelling or 1,200 square feet, whichever is less, but not less than 800 square feet. A Coast News report of the same update described that choice as "whichever is greater." The adopted ordinance says less. Use the ordinance.

Rule What CS-506 says
New attached ADU 50 percent of the main house or 1,200 sq ft, whichever is less, and not below 800 sq ft. This cap is for attached ADUs that add new square footage.
Conversion Must stay inside the existing or proposed house or accessory structure, and that structure still has to meet the zone's lot coverage.
Detached ADU 1,200 sq ft on a one-family lot. Size on two-family and multifamily lots follows Government Code section 66323.
JADU 500 sq ft, inside a one-family home, with a recorded deed restriction.
Detached height 16 feet. Within a half-mile walk of Carlsbad Village Station or Poinsettia Station, 18 feet, plus 2 feet (20 feet maximum) to match the main roof pitch. A detached ADU on a lot with a multistory two-family or multifamily building may be 18 feet.
Attached height 25 feet or the zone height for the main house, whichever is higher, and no more than two stories.
ADU over or under a detached garage Zone height. The ADU itself is one story. The garage and ADU together are two stories maximum.
Roof decks "Roof decks shall not be permitted on detached or attached ADUs."
Look of the building On a one-family lot, roofing, trim, walls, windows, and colors match the main house, except for ADUs that qualify under Government Code section 66323. The match cannot block the building code.

Setbacks and how many units

California ADU law stops a city from requiring more than 4 feet of side or rear setback. Carlsbad's ADU bulletin IB-111, listed on the city's bulletin index as last updated October 2025, describes that 4-foot side and rear minimum. The same bulletin says an 800 square foot ADU may use the front setback when the lot has no other place that works. A converted garage that already sits closer may keep its existing setbacks, unless it is in the coastal zone and the work affects coastal resources.

CS-506 does not print one flat unit count inside section 21.10.030. It points to Government Code sections 66314, 66321, 66323, and 66333. A Coast News report of the ordinance described a single-family lot as one JADU, one conversion ADU, and one attached or detached ADU. Ask Planning to apply the count to your lot. The coastal program can change what is buildable even when the state count looks open.

Coastal zone ADUs: the extra permit, and what got approved

If the lot is in the coastal zone, the ADU or JADU needs a coastal development permit under Chapter 21.201, plus a building permit. The city's Permit Ready page says that coastal approval is a Minor Coastal Development Permit, using the Minor CDP supplemental form and form P-1, and that it has to be approved before or at the same time as the ADU building permit. Attached, detached, and garage conversions are all in that sentence. An attached unit is not a way around the coastal permit.

The coastal portion of CS-506 is tied to LCPA 2025-0023. Those coastal-zone changes take effect only after the Coastal Commission approves the amendment, or 30 days after adoption, whichever is later. Check with the city before you design to the new coastal text. Habitat buffers, geologic setbacks, and view policies in the certified Local Coastal Program still apply. The ordinance says those coastal resource rules win if they conflict with a local development standard.

Replacement parking is not waived on every coastal lot

Statewide, converting or demolishing a garage for an ADU often means you do not replace that parking. Carlsbad keeps a local exception. You do replace the parking on lots west of the rail corridor, and on lots east of the rail corridor and west of Interstate 5 between Avenida Encinas on the north and Batiquitos Lagoon on the south. Where replacement is required, the spaces may be tandem, uncovered, or on a lift, in the front, side, or rear yard, on an improved surface. That strip is why a garage conversion on the west side of Carlsbad needs a parking plan, not a shrug.

Forty-five of the 46 notices state a unit size. The apartment legalization does not, so it is out of the size math. For the other 45, the median is 677 square feet. The smallest stated size is 367 square feet. The largest is 1,200. The split is 16 under 500 square feet, 9 from 500 to 749, 10 from 750 to 999, and 10 from 1,000 to 1,200. None is exactly 500. Twenty-five of the 45 are 750 square feet or smaller.

Five of those units sit in a tight band just under 500 square feet: 493, 497, 497, 497, and 498. There is no similar pile-up just under 750. We are not going to invent a Carlsbad fee line at 500 square feet. The pattern is real in the notices. Ask the city what changes at 500 and at 750 before you lock a floor plan around either number. California law is clear on one of them: impact fees are not charged on an ADU of 750 square feet or less. Carlsbad's own ADU page says many ADUs are exempt from impact fees. Permit Ready plans: the city has them, and they are paused

An AI summary that ranks for "custom ADU builder Carlsbad" says the city does not pre-approve plans. The city's Permit Ready page says the opposite. Carlsbad offers free permit-ready plans: four floor plans, three architectural styles, all single-story detached. The city says the plans can save three to six months and $8,000 to $16,000 in design cost.

Those plans are currently unavailable. The city is updating them to the 2025 California Building Code. As of September 29, 2026, the page still said the update was anticipated by summer 2026. It had not been revised to a new date. Forms tied to the program include the B-1 residential building permit application, the B-75 Permit Ready ADU checklist, and a hold harmless agreement.

Custom design is the live path right now. It is also the right path when the lot is coastal, the unit is attached or two stories, you are converting a garage, the size is outside those four plans, or the main house has a roof and window pattern the standard plans do not match. Our pre-approved ADU plans page covers how standard plans work in other San Diego jurisdictions, and what is still site-specific.

Even when Carlsbad turns the plans back on, a coastal lot still needs its own site plan and its own Minor CDP. A pre-checked floor plan does not measure your sewer route.

Carlsbad ADU cost: city numbers, then homeowner quotes

The same online summary says Carlsbad does not waive fees. The city's ADU page says many ADUs are exempt from impact fees, and that permit fees are approximately $2,000 to $4,000 depending on square footage. The page still points at the Master Fee Schedule for fiscal year 2024-25. The current citywide schedule is fiscal year 2026-27, approved June 16, 2026, and effective September 1, 2026. We are not quoting a line item out of that PDF. Get the fee quote from Building when the scope is known.

City permit fees

About $2,000 to $4,000, by size, on the city's ADU page. Impact fees are waived for many ADUs. Building permit and plan-check fees can still apply.

City construction range

The same page says about $10,000 for a simple bedroom conversion to about $300,000 for a higher-end companion unit. That is the city's published span, not a bid.

What owners are quoting

Local discussions mention about $250 to $350 per square foot, and one quote near $400 per square foot. Those are homeowner comments. They are not a fee schedule.

A homeowner in one of those threads told someone else to leave an extra $15,000 to $20,000 for things that show up after the first price. That is a sensible contingency for a coastal lot, where a survey, a soils letter, a longer sewer run, or a plan-check redraw can eat it. It is not a city fee, and it is not a promise that $20,000 covers a bad hillside or a panel upgrade.

Compare bids on the same scope: architecture, structural and MEP engineering, Title 24, the Minor CDP if you are coastal, plan-check fees, site work, utilities, construction, and what happens after the final inspection. A low square-foot price that stops at the shell is a different product. The countywide breakdown is on the ADU cost page. Rent math has the same jurisdiction problem. The City of San Diego requires a stay of at least 31 days for an ADU. Carlsbad writes its own rental rules. Where Carlsbad projects lose time

Owners describe the same stalls, without naming a single cause. Permit packages that take close to a year. A schedule that slips past the first quote, sometimes past a year from the first meeting. Emails that get no answer. A warranty item after move-in that sits.

The public record has a matching kind of delay, with the reason left blank. The Anderson ADU, file DEV2022-0175, received its Minor CDP notice on September 2, 2026. Four years between a file number and a coastal notice is long. The notice does not explain the gap. Use it as a reason to ask, in writing, who watches the file each week.

What the city controls

  • Plan check starts after fees are paid. An unpaid submittal is not in line.
  • Several building permits can be filed online. Hard copies need an appointment. Walk-ins are not allowed.
  • A coastal ADU needs the Minor CDP before or with the building permit.
  • The city owes a decision inside the Government Code section 66317 clock, and a written correction list if it says no.

What usually burns the clock

  • Incomplete drawings, so the first city letter is a list, not an approval.
  • A design that ignores the coastal buffer, the view policy, or the parking strip west of the tracks.
  • No one assigned to answer corrections. The file waits on the applicant.
  • A builder site that still says every Carlsbad permit is in-person only. The city page says several permits are online. Follow the city page.

City process: building permits. City phones, which are the city's and not ours: Building permits 442-339-2719, inspections 442-339-2700, building@carlsbadca.gov. Planning 442-339-2610, planning@carlsbadca.gov.

What Carlsbad homeowners say about ADU builders

These are themes from public reviews and local ADU-group comments about firms that work in and around Carlsbad. Review text is often short, so this is the pattern, not a scoreboard. We are not naming or ranking other builders.

What goes wrong

Permits that drag toward a year. Projects that run past the advertised schedule. Emails with no reply. Warranty calls after the keys are handed over that get little or no response. A first price that did not include the extras, which is why owners tell each other to hold back $15,000 to $20,000.

What owners praise

Steady communication. One team that stays on the paperwork and submits the permit in the owner's name. A finished unit described as on time and on budget. A builder who talked the owner out of choices that only added cost, and who said the limits of the lot up front.

What to ignore

A map pin that says "Carlsbad ADU builder" and a star rating with no coastal permit in the story. Ask for a Carlsbad Minor CDP the firm actually filed. A kitchen remodel review does not answer that question.

A checklist for any builder

  • Who is the single contact, and what response time is written into the contract?
  • Who pulls the permit: you, or the builder on your behalf?
  • Have they filed a Carlsbad Minor CDP, or only inland building permits?
  • Is the price itemized for design, structural and MEP engineering, coastal forms, city fees, construction, and closeout?
  • Which site items are allowances, and which are fixed?
  • What is the written warranty path after final inspection, including who answers?
  • What happens, on the calendar and on the invoice, when plan check sends corrections?

Questions Carlsbad owners actually ask

Is there a company that handles a Carlsbad ADU from plans through construction, and is it expensive?

A design-build team can take a Carlsbad ADU from the site check through plans, city submittal, and construction. The city publishes a construction range from about $10,000 for a simple bedroom conversion to about $300,000 for a higher-end companion unit. Homeowners in local discussions have mentioned quotes around $250 to $400 per square foot. Those are anecdotes, not a city price list. Compare an itemized scope before you treat any square-foot number as the project cost.

How do I find an ADU designer who works in Carlsbad's coastal area?

Ask whether the designer or design-build firm has prepared Carlsbad Minor Coastal Development Permits, not only inland building permits. An ADU in the coastal zone needs a Minor Coastal Development Permit approved before or at the same time as the building permit. Ask who fills out the coastal forms and whose name goes on the application.

Who handles design, permitting, and exterior work for a two-story attached ADU in Carlsbad?

One team can handle the design, the permit set, and the exterior construction. Under Ordinance CS-506, a new attached ADU is limited to 50 percent of the main house or 1,200 square feet, whichever is less, and the city cannot force that new attached unit below 800 square feet. Height can reach 25 feet or the zone height limit, whichever is higher, with a two-story cap. Roof decks are not allowed. In the coastal zone, an attached ADU still needs a Minor Coastal Development Permit. Coastal notices from 2024 to 2026 include attached units of 677, 796, 800, and 916 square feet. On a two-story plan, also check ordinary livability: a local commenter flagged a sketch whose only bathroom sat inside the upstairs bedroom, and whose main bedroom had no closet. Those fail daily use even when the height math is legal.

What are the loft rules for a Carlsbad ADU, and can I use a ladder instead of stairs?

Carlsbad enforces the California Building Standards Code. A sleeping loft has to meet the ceiling height, guard, and access rules in the code edition the city is using. A ladder is allowed only when that adopted code allows it for that loft. A pull-down attic ladder is not an automatic substitute for stairs. The city's Permit Ready plans are paused while they are updated to the 2025 California Building Code, so the code edition on the drawings matters. Show the loft, the ceiling heights, and the stair or ladder before you submit.

Do I need a separate draftsman, plan runner, and MEP team, or does the builder provide them?

You can hire a draftsman, a plan runner, and a mechanical, electrical, and plumbing team separately. A design-build contract can also include drafting, permit submittal, and that engineering. Ask which of those items are in the price, who stamps the plans, and who answers the city's correction letter.

Is $400 per square foot a normal ADU quote?

About $400 per square foot has shown up in local homeowner discussions as one person's quote. It is not a Carlsbad city fee. It can be a plausible all-in number for a custom unit once design, engineering, permits, site work, and finishes are included. It can also leave those items out. Compare itemized bids, not the square-foot headline. Small units often cost more per square foot because the kitchen, bath, and permit work are spread across fewer feet. The cost guide breaks that down.

Does the contractor include permits and engineering for a garage-to-ADU conversion in Carlsbad?

Ask for that in writing. A complete garage-conversion price should say whether it includes the building permit submittal, structural engineering, Title 24 energy paperwork, and, if the garage is in the coastal zone, the Minor Coastal Development Permit. Utility upgrades and slab repairs are the items most often missing from a low number. Confirm the Carlsbad coastal step separately from a general garage conversion checklist.

Do I need plans before I start with a builder?

You do not need a finished plan set before the first meeting. You do need plans before Carlsbad will issue the building permit. Start with a feasibility check of jurisdiction, coastal zone, setbacks, and utilities, then draw the unit that fits that lot. That first pass is what our feasibility study is for.
