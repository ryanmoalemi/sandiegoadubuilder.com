ADU Cost in San Diego

The real ADU cost includes everything required to reach final inspection.

A useful San Diego ADU budget includes feasibility, plans, engineering, permits, site work, utilities, construction, finishes, financing costs, contingency and restoration. A low square-foot price that excludes these items is not the cost to complete the project.

San Diego ADU pricing cannot be reduced to one number. A 600-square-foot flat-lot unit in Clairemont with a short sewer connection is a different project from a 600-square-foot unit on a Point Loma slope, a coastal parcel in La Jolla or a septic property in Ramona.

Planning range, not a quote: complete detached ADUs in San Diego are frequently discussed in a broad range of roughly $375 to $600 or more per square foot, with many total projects around $300,000 to $450,000 or higher. Small, difficult, luxury, coastal and hillside projects can exceed these ranges.

The following figures are broad planning ranges for complete projects, not bids. Actual pricing requires a property review, defined plans, engineering, utility scope and contractor proposal.

Do not compare project types by total price alone. A conversion uses existing structure while a detached ADU requires a new foundation, exterior walls, roof, complete building envelope and independent site connections.

Why each ADU type has a different cost structure

1

Detached ADU

Includes a complete new building plus site work. It usually provides the best privacy and rental separation but carries the highest foundation, exterior, roofing and utility cost.

2

Attached ADU

Shares part of the main structure, but tie-in work can be expensive. Costs rise when the roof, foundation, framing, electrical service or occupied rooms must be modified.

3

Garage conversion

Saves the shell only if that shell is legally permitted and suitable for habitation. Many garages need slab, moisture, insulation, fire, framing, window and utility upgrades.

4

JADU

Uses space inside a single-family home and can reduce construction cost. The owner sacrifices primary-home area and may still need separation, egress, kitchen, utility and access work.

5

Second story

Preserves yard area but adds stairs, structural reinforcement, temporary protection, roof removal and more labor around an occupied building.

6

Prefab or modular ADU

Factory production may control building costs, but the quoted unit price often excludes design, permits, foundation, crane, transportation, utilities, site work and finish connections.

Design, engineering and preconstruction costs

Feasibility study

Reviews the parcel, jurisdiction, zoning, buildable envelope, utilities, fire, coastal, drainage and likely permit path before full design.

Boundary and topographic survey

Becomes important when the ADU is close to setbacks, easements, slopes, retaining walls or uncertain property lines.

Architectural plans

Include site plan, floor plans, elevations, sections, details, code information and coordination with consultants.

Structural engineering

Covers foundation, framing, shear walls, roof, retaining conditions and calculations based on the selected design.

Energy documentation

Title 24 work addresses envelope, windows, HVAC, ventilation, water heating and applicable solar requirements.

Special consultants

Geotechnical, civil, septic, fire, arborist, historic, coastal or other specialists may be required by the property.

Low design fees can increase construction cost. Missing utility coordination, sections, details or existing-condition work often returns as change orders and field delays.

Utility costs can move the budget by tens of thousands

Utility cost is determined by distance, elevation, capacity, access, trench conditions and the rules of the serving agency. The cheapest building location is not always the cheapest utility location.

  • Length and depth of sewer trench
  • Gravity flow versus sewage ejector pump
  • Existing sewer lateral condition and capacity
  • Water meter, pressure and supply route
  • Electrical panel and service capacity
  • SDG&E coordination and equipment location
  • Gas removal or extension where applicable
  • Hardscape, landscaping and wall restoration
  • Utility conflicts and required separations
  • Separate metering requested by the owner

Site, grading and foundation cost factors

Demolition

Sheds, patios, walls, trees, pools, old garages and undocumented structures can require abatement, permits and disposal.

Excavation and export

Soil volume, truck access, disposal, contaminated soil, rock and hand excavation affect cost.

Retaining walls

Engineering, drainage, waterproofing, shoring, property-line conditions and access make retaining work expensive.

Foundation type

Slab-on-grade is generally simpler than raised, stepped, deep or pier foundation systems required by the site.

Drainage

Roof runoff, surface grading, subdrains, pumps and protection of neighboring property must be resolved.

Construction access

Narrow side yards, stairs, occupied homes, pools and walls increase hand labor, pump distance and staging cost.

ADU permit and impact fees

Government fees are not one fixed San Diego number. They depend on whether the property is in the City of San Diego, unincorporated County or another city, plus project valuation, review type, size, utilities and special permits.

Building and plan-review fees

These may include planning, building, structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, records, inspections and other agency charges.

Impact-fee rule

California law prohibits impact fees for ADUs with 750 square feet or less of interior livable space. Larger ADUs may be charged proportionately under state law.

Other possible charges

Utility, fire, school, grading, right-of-way, septic, coastal and special-district costs may follow separate rules and are not automatically eliminated.

County update: the County of San Diego's trial ADU impact-fee waiver ended January 9, 2024. Current County and outside-agency fees should be verified for the parcel and project.

How San Diego location changes ADU cost

Clairemont and Mira Mesa

Many relatively flat suburban lots support efficient detached construction, but sewer location, aging electrical service, slab elevation and access still vary.

North Park and central neighborhoods

Tight lots, alleys, older garages, historic questions, undocumented additions and limited staging can increase design and labor costs.

La Jolla and coastal communities

Coastal review, restricted access, premium exterior expectations, corrosion exposure and high-value landscaping can raise total project cost.

Point Loma and canyon properties

Grade, retaining walls, fire conditions, drainage and difficult concrete delivery can create substantial site costs.

Chula Vista, La Mesa and Santee

Suburban lots may provide good construction access and standard-plan fit, but local permit agencies and utility conditions must be priced separately.

Ramona, Alpine and rural County

Septic, wells, fire access, grading, long utility runs and distance from labor or suppliers can outweigh the advantage of a larger lot.

Ways to control ADU cost without reducing safety or durability

Complete feasibility first

Choose the building area around sewer, grade, access and fire constraints before paying for full plans.

Use a simple footprint

Rectangular plans, aligned walls and simple roof geometry reduce framing, flashing, waste and engineering.

Place wet rooms efficiently

Cluster the kitchen, bathroom and laundry near the best utility connection.

Limit structural changes

For conversions and attached units, work with existing framing instead of forcing a layout that requires major reinforcement.

Use standard openings

Standard windows and doors generally cost less and simplify structural framing and replacement.

Select durable mid-range finishes

Spend on waterproofing, envelope, systems and installation quality before decorative upgrades.

Preserve access

Plan construction routes before final placement to reduce hand labor, crane work and property damage.

Price from complete plans

A detailed plan set creates more comparable contractor bids and fewer allowances.

Make decisions before construction

Late changes create demolition, redesign, schedule delay and rush purchasing.

Do not save money in the wrong places

  • Waterproofing and flashing
  • Drainage and grading
  • Structural connections
  • Electrical capacity and safety
  • Plumbing below slabs or inside walls
  • Fire assemblies
  • Ventilation and moisture control
  • Permit and inspection compliance

Value engineering should remove waste, not required performance

The best cost reductions simplify geometry, reduce unnecessary finishes, shorten utility runs and eliminate redundant scope. Cutting concealed building systems can create leaks, mold, electrical problems and expensive repairs after the ADU is occupied.

Common ADU cost mistakes

Using square footage alone

Cost per square foot hides utilities, slope, access and fixed kitchen and bathroom costs.

Comparing shell price to turnkey price

One proposal includes foundation, permits and utilities while another includes only the building.

No site allowance

The budget assumes the backyard is empty and flat despite walls, patios, trees, pools or grade.

Ignoring the sewer

The selected location requires deep trenching or pumping that was not included in early pricing.

No electrical review

An all-electric unit is designed before panel and service capacity are checked.

Using an outdated permit estimate

Fees and agency requirements are copied from another city, an old project or a general online calculator.

Underpricing conversions

The garage shell is assumed usable before slab, moisture, framing, roof and permit records are verified.

No construction access plan

Concrete, excavation and materials must be moved by hand through a narrow side yard.

Spending contingency on finishes

Optional upgrades use the reserve before underground utilities and inspections are complete.

Changing the plan after pricing

Added windows, bathrooms, roof changes and square footage make the original estimate obsolete.

Excluding financing cost

Interest, appraisal, title, inspections and draw fees are omitted from the project return analysis.

Ignoring post-construction costs

Landscaping, fencing, insurance, utilities, maintenance and rental turnover are not included.

ADU cost and investment return

An ADU should not be evaluated using gross rent divided by construction cost alone. The owner should compare the complete project capital, financing payment, operating expenses, vacancy, maintenance, insurance, taxes and the value of the unit's non-rental use.

San Diego ADU cost FAQs

How much does a detached ADU cost in San Diego?

Many complete detached projects broadly fall around $300,000 to $450,000 or higher. A small flat-lot project may fall below that range, while coastal, hillside, luxury and complex utility projects can exceed $500,000.

What is a realistic cost per square foot?

A broad complete-project planning range for detached San Diego ADUs is approximately $375 to $600 or more per square foot. This metric becomes less reliable for small units and properties with major fixed site costs.

How much does a 500-square-foot ADU cost?

A broad detached planning range is approximately $225,000 to $350,000 or more, depending on utilities, access, foundation, plans, permits and finishes.

How much does a 750-square-foot ADU cost?

A complete detached 750-square-foot San Diego ADU may broadly range from approximately $275,000 to $425,000 or more. Difficult properties can exceed this range.

How much does a 1,200-square-foot ADU cost?

A complete project may broadly range from approximately $400,000 to $600,000 or more, depending on site, architecture, number of bathrooms, structural work and finish level.

Why are San Diego ADUs expensive?

San Diego costs reflect skilled labor, code compliance, kitchens and bathrooms, energy systems, utilities, site constraints, permits, fire or coastal conditions and work on occupied residential properties.

Is a garage conversion always cheaper?

No. It is cheaper only when the existing garage is permitted and structurally useful. Foundation, moisture, framing, roofing, fire and utility corrections can make a conversion expensive.

Is a JADU the least expensive option?

It can be because it uses existing interior space, but the main house may require separation, access, kitchen, utility, egress and layout changes.

Are pre-approved ADU plans cheaper?

They can reduce original architectural design and review time when the plan fits the property. Site plans, licensing, foundation, utilities, engineering and permit costs remain.

How much do ADU plans cost?

Total design and engineering cost varies by project. A broad planning assumption may be roughly 7% to 15% of total project cost, with difficult or highly custom work potentially exceeding that range.

How much are ADU permits in San Diego?

There is no single fixed amount. Fees depend on the agency, valuation, size, review disciplines, utilities and special permits. Obtain a project-specific estimate from the responsible jurisdiction.

Are ADUs under 750 square feet exempt from all fees?

No. California law prohibits impact fees for qualifying ADUs with 750 square feet or less of interior livable space. Building permit, plan review, utility and other costs can still apply.

Did the County of San Diego waive ADU fees?

The County's trial impact-fee waiver ended January 9, 2024. Current County and outside-agency costs should be verified for the parcel.

How much should I budget for utilities?

Utility costs can range from tens of thousands to substantially more. Sewer elevation, trench length, panel capacity, service upgrades, hardscape and utility-company work determine the actual amount.

Does solar add to ADU cost?

New detached ADUs may have applicable energy and solar requirements depending on project conditions and current code. Solar design, electrical service and interconnection should be included early.

How much contingency should I include?

The appropriate reserve depends on project risk. New flat-lot construction with complete plans requires less than a garage conversion, hillside project or property with uncertain utilities and undocumented construction.

Can I build an ADU for $100,000?

A small interior JADU or unusually straightforward conversion may approach that budget. A complete new detached San Diego ADU generally cannot be delivered for $100,000 after plans, permits, foundation, utilities and finishes.

Are prefab ADUs less expensive?

Factory production may reduce building-shell cost, but the owner must compare the complete installed project, including foundation, transport, crane, permits, utilities and site work.

Does a second-story ADU save money?

It saves yard area, not necessarily money. Structural reinforcement, stairs, roof removal, occupied-home protection and access can make second-story construction expensive.

Can I reduce cost by acting as owner-builder?

It may reduce general-contractor markup for a qualified and experienced owner, but it transfers scheduling, safety, insurance, trade coordination, quality, lien and inspection responsibility to the owner.

Do premium finishes greatly change the total?

Yes. Cabinets, windows, doors, tile, plumbing fixtures, appliances and custom millwork can add tens of thousands. Structural and utility costs generally should be resolved before finish upgrades.

Will an ADU increase property value by its cost?

Not automatically. Value depends on legality, quality, privacy, neighborhood, rental utility and comparable sales. Construction cost and market value are different.

What should I do before requesting bids?

Complete feasibility, select the ADU type, define utilities, prepare coordinated plans and issue the same scope to each contractor. Otherwise the bids will not be comparable.

What is the biggest hidden ADU cost?

Utilities and site conditions are common hidden costs. Sewer, electrical service, excavation, retaining walls and access can add far more than expected from looking at the open yard.
